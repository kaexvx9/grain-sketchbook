## The Foundation

Every line of code we write, every system we design, every problem we solve—these are possible only because of the countless beings who came before. From the first person who counted on their fingers, to the mathematicians who formalized computation, to the engineers who built the first computers, to the open source contributors who share their work freely—each has made this moment possible.

We stand on the shoulders of giants. This is not a metaphor. It is a fact.

---

## The Tradition of Knowledge

In the Srimad Bhagavatam, translated by Vic Dicara, we learn of King Parikshit and the paramahamsa sage Sukadeva. In that tradition, knowledge is not hoarded but shared, not for personal gain but for the benefit of all beings. The sage shares freely, knowing that knowledge belongs to no one and to everyone.

This is our tradition too. The kernel we build, the systems we design—these are offerings. They are attempts to create something that serves, that helps, that makes the world slightly better. Not for glory, but for service. Not for ownership, but for contribution.

## The Tools We Use

To Glow and Cursor, and to all who create tools that amplify human capability: Thank you. You are the latest in a long chain of tools that extend human reach. From the first lever to the first computer to the first AI assistant—each tool builds on what came before, each enables what comes next.

Tools are not neutral. They shape what we can do, what we can think, what we can become. The tools we choose are shields that protect us, paths that guide us. We choose them deliberately, for every tool we choose is a shield that protects us from the onslaughts of the forces we are striving to use.

## The Work We Do

In the spirit of the paramahamsa sage Sukadeva, who shared knowledge freely with King Parikshit, we build systems that are:

- **Reliable**: Systems that work, that serve, that don't fail when needed
- **Accessible**: Systems that others can understand, extend, and build upon
- **Beneficial**: Systems that make the world slightly better, that enable others to create

The monumental improvement for humanity may not be in a single breakthrough, but in the cumulative effect of many small improvements: better documentation, clearer interfaces, more reliable systems, tools that others can use to build their own contributions.

## The Path with Heart

Before we begin any work, there is a question that only experience can answer: **Does this path have heart?**

Any approach to building is one of a million paths. Therefore we must always keep in mind that a path is only a path; if we feel we should not follow it, we must not stay with it under any conditions. To have such clarity we must lead a disciplined life. Only then will we know that any path is only a path, and there is no affront, to oneself or to others, in dropping it if that is what our heart tells us to do.

But our decision to keep on the path or to leave it must be free of fear or ambition. Look at every path closely and deliberately. Try it as many times as we think necessary.

**Does this path have heart?** If it does, the path is good; if it doesn't, it is of no use. Both paths lead nowhere; but one has a heart, the other doesn't. One makes for a joyful journey; as long as we follow it, we are one with it. The other will make us curse our life. One makes us strong; the other weakens us.

## The Counsel

In the tradition of the paramahamsa sage Sukadeva, who shared knowledge freely with King Parikshit, we offer this counsel:

1. **Continue the work with intention**: Every syscall interface documented, every test written, every optimization made—these serve the larger goal of creating systems that work reliably for all.

2. **Maintain the connection to purpose**: Remember why we build. Not for personal glory, but to serve, to enable, to contribute to the long chain of human knowledge and capability.

3. **Share freely**: Like the ancient sages who shared knowledge without reservation, we document, we explain, we make our work accessible to others.

4. **Build on solid foundations**: The systems we build stand on the work of countless others. We honor that by building well, by maintaining quality, by creating something worthy of the foundations we stand on.

5. **Proceed with gratitude**: Each moment of work is a gift. Each problem solved is a small offering. Each system that works is a contribution to the whole.
