## Part One: What is Modern Monetary Theory? (ELI5)

### Chapter 1: Money is Not What You Think

Imagine you are playing a game with your friends. You are the banker. You have a special power: you can create as much game money as you need.

That is basically what a country can do with its own money. The United States can create as many dollars as it needs. It does not need to borrow dollars from anyone else first.

This is the first big idea of Modern Monetary Theory: **A country that controls its own money can always create more of that money.**

### Chapter 2: The Old Story vs The New Story

**The Old Story** (what most people believe):
- Countries need to collect taxes first before they can spend money
- Countries need to borrow money before they can spend it
- If a country spends too much, it will go broke
- Working people need to pay taxes to fund government programs

**The New Story** (Modern Monetary Theory):
- Countries create money when they spend it
- Taxes are not needed to fund spending (they are needed for other reasons)
- The real limit is not money, but resources (workers, materials, time)
- If a country spends too much, it causes inflation (prices go up), not bankruptcy

### Chapter 3: Why Taxes Matter (But Not For The Reason You Think)

If countries can create money, why do they collect taxes?

Taxes do three important things:

1. **Control Inflation**: When too much money is in the economy, prices go up. Taxes take money out of the economy, which helps keep prices stable.

2. **Encourage Good Behavior**: Taxes can make people do things we want (like use less pollution) or stop doing things we do not want (like smoke cigarettes).

3. **Redistribute Wealth**: Taxes can take money from rich people and use it to help poor people.

**The key insight**: Taxes are not needed to pay for government programs. They are needed to control the economy and make it fair.

### Chapter 4: The Problem with Sales Tax

Sales tax is a tax you pay when you buy something. If you buy a shirt for ten dollars and sales tax is ten percent, you pay eleven dollars total.

Here is the problem: **Sales tax hurts poor people more than rich people.**

Why? Because poor people spend almost all their money on things they need (food, clothes, housing). Rich people save a lot of their money. So poor people pay sales tax on almost all their money, while rich people pay sales tax on only part of their money.

This is called a "regressive tax" because it goes backwards - it hurts the people who have less money.

### Chapter 5: The Solution: Progressive Income Tax

Income tax is a tax you pay on the money you earn from your job.

A "progressive" income tax means:
- People who earn less money pay a lower percentage
- People who earn more money pay a higher percentage

For example:
- If you earn thirty thousand dollars a year, you might pay five percent
- If you earn three hundred thousand dollars a year, you might pay ten percent

This is fair because rich people can afford to pay more, and they usually save more of their money anyway.

### Chapter 6: Why This Matters for Working People

From a Modern Monetary Theory perspective, here is what we want:

1. **Working people should keep most of their money** (so they can spend it and help the economy)
2. **Rich people should pay more taxes** (because they save their money, not spend it)
3. **We should not have sales tax** (because it hurts working people)

This creates what economists call "effective demand" - when working people have money, they spend it, which creates jobs and helps everyone.

## Part Two: Why Oregon is the Best State for MMT

### Chapter 7: The Five States with No Sales Tax

There are only five states in America that do not have a sales tax:
1. Alaska
2. Delaware
3. Montana
4. New Hampshire
5. Oregon

This is already good for working people! No sales tax means you keep more of your money when you buy things.

But which of these five states is the best for working people and people who move here from other countries?

### Chapter 8: Oregon's Special Tax System

Oregon has:
- **No sales tax** (good for working people)
- **Progressive income tax** (rich people pay more, working people pay less)
- **Lower property tax** than many other states

This is the perfect combination for Modern Monetary Theory!

Here is why:
- Working people keep most of their money (no sales tax, lower income tax rates)
- Rich people pay more taxes (higher income tax rates)
- This creates effective demand (working people spend money, which helps the economy)

### Chapter 9: Oregon vs Other States

Let's compare Oregon to other states:

**Alaska**: Has no sales tax and no income tax, which is great! But it has a special program called the Permanent Fund Dividend that gives everyone money each year. However, Alaska is very expensive to live in, and the weather is harsh.

**Delaware**: Has no sales tax and the lowest property tax in America. But it still has income tax on working people, which reduces how much money they can spend.

**Montana**: Has no sales tax and the cheapest housing. But it has income tax on working people, and it is not very friendly to people who move here from other countries.

**New Hampshire**: Has no sales tax and no income tax on wages. But it has the highest property tax of these five states, and it is not very friendly to people who move here from other countries.

**Oregon**: Has no sales tax, progressive income tax (targets rich people), lower property tax, and is very friendly to people who move here from other countries. It also has lots of job opportunities.

**Winner: Oregon!**

### Chapter 10: Why Oregon is Better Than States with No Income Tax

Some states have no income tax at all (like Texas, Florida, Nevada, Washington). This sounds great, right?

But here is the problem: **These states have high sales taxes instead.**

For example:
- Texas has no income tax, but sales tax is over eight percent
- Florida has no income tax, but sales tax is seven and a half percent
- Washington has no income tax, but sales tax is almost ten percent

Remember: Sales tax hurts working people more than rich people.

So even though these states have no income tax, working people still pay a lot in taxes through sales tax.

**Oregon is better because**: It has no sales tax (which helps working people) and progressive income tax (which targets rich people, not working people).

### Chapter 11: Oregon's Other Advantages

Oregon is not just good because of taxes. It also has:

1. **Friendly policies for people who move here**: Oregon has "sanctuary cities" that protect people who move here from other countries. This helps them feel safe and participate in the economy.

2. **Lots of job opportunities**: Oregon needs workers in farming, construction, technology, and healthcare. There are not enough workers for all the jobs available.

3. **Reasonable cost of living**: While not the cheapest state, Oregon is more affordable than Alaska or New Hampshire.

4. **Beautiful places to live**: Oregon has mountains, forests, coastlines, and cities. There is something for everyone.

## Part Five: Grain Court Karma for Oregon

### Chapter 21: What is Grain Court Karma?

Grain Court Karma is a cloud computing system (software that runs on the internet) designed to help Oregon's state government run better.

**Cloud computing** means software and data stored on the internet, accessible from anywhere.

**Grain Court Karma** is designed to:
- Help Oregon's government make decisions
- Run programs like the Job Guarantee (a program that ensures everyone who wants a job can get one)
- Manage the state budget and currency (called $WAVE)
- Connect different parts of government together
- Make government more transparent and fair

### Chapter 22: What is a Constitution DAO?

A **DAO** stands for "Decentralized Autonomous Organization." This is a fancy way of saying: **A group of people who make decisions together using computer code and voting.**

A **Constitution DAO** means the rules for how decisions are made are based on a constitution (like the U.S. Constitution or Oregon's state constitution).

Here is how it works:
1. People propose ideas (like "should we fund this program?")
2. People vote on the ideas
3. If enough people vote yes, the idea becomes a rule
4. Computer code automatically carries out the rule

This makes government more:
- **Transparent**: Everyone can see what decisions are being made
- **Fair**: Everyone gets a vote (based on how the constitution says voting should work)
- **Efficient**: Computer code handles the boring parts automatically

### Chapter 23: How Grain Court Karma Helps Oregon

Grain Court Karma would help Oregon in many ways:

1. **Job Guarantee Program**: The system could automatically match people who want jobs with available work, pay them in $WAVE currency, and track their progress.

2. **Budget Management**: The system could help Oregon manage its budget, track spending, and make sure money goes where it is needed.

3. **$WAVE Currency**: Oregon could have its own digital currency called $WAVE. This currency would be designed to encourage economic participation (the same USD buys more $WAVE over time, encouraging people to use it).

4. **Transparency**: All government decisions and spending would be visible to everyone, making government more accountable.

5. **Efficiency**: Computer code would handle routine tasks, freeing up government workers to focus on helping people.

### Chapter 24: How This Connects to Modern Monetary Theory

Remember Modern Monetary Theory? The idea that states can create their own money and use it to fund programs?

Grain Court Karma would help Oregon do this better:

1. **Create $WAVE Currency**: Oregon could create $WAVE currency to fund programs like the Job Guarantee.

2. **Manage Inflation**: The system could automatically adjust the supply of $WAVE to keep prices stable.

3. **Track Economic Activity**: The system could track how $WAVE is being used, helping Oregon make better economic decisions.

4. **Run Programs Automatically**: Programs like the Job Guarantee could run automatically, matching people with jobs and paying them.

This would make Oregon's MMT-based economy work better and more fairly.

### Chapter 25: Privacy and Security

You might be worried: "If the government runs everything on computers, won't they track everything I do?"

Grain Court Karma is designed with privacy in mind:
- Your personal data is encrypted (scrambled so only you can read it)

The goal is to make government work better while still respecting your privacy.
