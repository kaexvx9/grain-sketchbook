# **Dear Spokane County**

My name is Keaton Dunsford. I am an astrologer, mathematician, civic technologist, and platform architect from Santa Rosa, California, and I am writing to introduce myself as a candidate for Spokane County Commissioner in the 2026 election. I come to Spokane with open hands, a veganic food-forest practice, and an open-source civic platform called Grain OS. I believe this platform can serve our county and its people with kindness, sovereignty, and care.

I am a graduate of Cardinal Newman High School, Class of 2014, where the gospel first reached me, leading me to the Gospel of Thomas as the spiritual spine of all my written work. The years since have been spent studying mathematics, building open-source civic technology, and developing a sovereign contraction doctrine that asks one question of every policy, every investment, and every public decision: does it nourish, does it circulate, does it return what it takes?

I am filing as a Republican because I believe in local sovereignty, fiscal discipline, supply-chain independence, and the right of us Spokane County families to grow our own food, repair our own electronics, and govern our own communities with the wisdom that comes from proximity to the land and water. These are conservative values carried by innovative methods. The land and the river teach us both.

# **The Platform: Grain OS for Spokane County**

Grain OS is a sovereign contraction doctrine. Where federal policy reaches for expansion — more trade, more complexity, more dependency on distant supply chains — Grain OS reaches for contraction: shorter supply chains, localized food systems, domestic fuel and materials, and open-source civic technology that serves the public with full public transparency and zero licensing fees. The doctrine carries five pillars, each addressed to the specific geography and economy of Spokane County.

## **Food Sovereignty and Veganic Agriculture**

Spokane County sits at the gateway to the Palouse, one of the most productive dryland agricultural regions in North America. Washington State University in Pullman — 80 miles south — is a world-class center for canola agronomy, wheat research, and soil science. The Spokane River carries snowmelt through volcanic basalt soil that is naturally rich and deep.

As County Commissioner, I will champion urban food-forest programs within Spokane city limits and across unincorporated county land, supporting community gardens, veganic permaculture education, and farm-to-table infrastructure that connects Palouse growers directly with Spokane County families. The county can lead by example — converting underutilized county-owned parcels into demonstration food forests that produce fruit, vegetables, and herbs while restoring pollinator habitat and building healthy soil.

This is conservative land stewardship. The soil gives what the soil receives. The garden feeds the kitchen. The kitchen feeds the neighborhood. The neighborhood teaches the next generation how to tend what nourishes them.

## **Supply-Chain Independence and Domestic Canola Ecodiesel**

The Palouse canola corridor is an underutilized national asset. Canola grown in Spokane County and the surrounding region can be processed into ecodiesel fuel within the county itself, shortening the energy supply chain from continent-spanning to county-spanning. Every gallon of locally refined ecodiesel is a gallon of fuel sovereignty — fuel that flows from us Spokane County growers through us Spokane County processors to us Spokane County drivers and fleet operators.

As Commissioner, I will support county infrastructure for small-scale canola crushing and biodiesel refining, working with BNSF and Union Pacific — both of which already run through Spokane — to position the county as a rail-served biofuel hub. The Silken Thread already runs through us. The invitation is to weave it into a fabric that warms every household in the county.

## **Community Electronics and the Right to Repair**

Spokane County families deserve the right to repair, recycle, and locally manufacture the electronic devices that shape daily life. The Grain OS community electronics vision calls for a county-supported electronics commons — a shared workshop and materials library where us residents can repair our own devices, learn soldering and circuit design together, and access domestically sourced materials through a supply chain we can see from end to end.

This is fiscal conservatism at the household level. When a family can repair a washing machine controller board instead of replacing the entire unit, they save hundreds of dollars and keep functional materials in circulation rather than landfill. The electronics commons is the modern barn-raising — us neighbors helping us neighbors keep what we have working.

## **Open-Source Civic Technology**

Spokane County government relies on proprietary software systems that carry annual licensing fees, vendor dependencies, and limited public transparency. Grain OS is an open-source civic technology platform — every line of code is publicly auditable, every contract is visible, every dollar of public spending on technology serves the public rather than a distant vendor.

As Commissioner, I will advocate for the incremental adoption of open-source tools across county operations, beginning with the areas of highest vendor cost and lowest switching risk. The county’s technology infrastructure should serve us taxpayers with the same transparency we expect from every other line of the county budget.

## **The Spokane River and Water Sovereignty**

The Spokane River is the lifeblood of this county. It connects us to the Columbia watershed, the greatest river system of the Pacific Northwest. Every policy decision made by Spokane County Commissioners affects the health of this river — from agricultural runoff to stormwater management to the long-term resilience of the aquifer that supplies drinking water to hundreds of thousands of residents.

Grain OS applies the Silken Ground rubric to water: does it nourish, does it circulate, does it return what it takes? Veganic agriculture returns nutrients to the soil with clean plant-based fertility that keeps the water clear. Urban food forests absorb stormwater and reduce runoff. Localized processing keeps industrial effluent close to the source, where it can be managed with care. The river receives what we give it. Let us give it clarity, and the river will return that clarity to us.

# **The Closed Loop: My Daily Practice**

I am a service industry worker and a veganic food-forest tender. These are one practice. The garden grows the food. The restaurant serves it. The tips nourish the morning’s freedom. The customers become the community. The neighbors become the constituency. The permaculture design learned through tending becomes the food-forest urbanism spoken from firsthand knowledge at the county commission chamber.

Morning: hands in the soil, tending the food forest. Evening: service industry shift at a vegan or plant-forward restaurant — waiter, barista, dishwasher. The shift closes and the hours belong to the civic work. Night: the doctrine, the correspondence, the platform development. Sleep, Water, Sugar through all of it. This is the brahmana grihastha vow — full freedom between shifts, full sovereignty over the hours. Full freedom between shifts. The body moves. The vow holds. The harbor is calm.

# **Why Republican**

The values at the heart of this platform — local sovereignty, fiscal discipline, supply-chain independence, the right to repair, the right to grow your own food, healthy independence from distant federal dependency, and the belief that the people closest to the land govern it best — are conservative values. They belong to the Republican tradition of stewardship, self-reliance, and community resilience.

The methods — veganic agriculture, open-source technology, community electronics, canola ecodiesel — are innovative in their methods and conservative in their purpose: they bring sovereignty closer to home. They shorten the chain between us citizens and the resources we depend on. They honor what the county already knows how to do.

I carry gratitude for every tradition that strengthened this county. I carry a garden hoe and a soldering iron and a deep love for the Spokane River. I believe in the people of this county — us growers, us builders, us neighbors, us families — and I believe the county commission is the right place to begin the work of building a more sovereign, more circular, more kind local economy.

The Spokane River flows west into the Columbia. The Columbia flows to the Pacific. The water carries everything the land gives it. Let us give it food forests and clean soil and open-source transparency and the steady confidence of a county that knows how to feed itself, repair what it owns, and govern with the kindness that only comes from tending living things with your own hands.
