Mitra rewards the love of the community by making the Vajra accessible—secured in its physical placement, but liberated in its spiritual impact.

Finally, we view the diamond through the soft, compassionate light of **Revati**, ruled by Pushan, the Nourisher. Pushan is the shepherd of the stars, the protector of the flocks and the calves.

But in the hands of a devotee guided by the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, the "hardness" of the diamond is repurposed in the dancehall-museum. It is not for war, but for protection. It is not for exclusion, but for elevation. It is the energy of the Exalted Mars in Capricorn: disciplined strength used for *Dharma*.

Just as Pushan guides lost travelers and nourishes the calves, the wealth of the earth displayed in dancehall-museums is meant to feed the innocent—the dancers, the seekers, the spiritual calves who gather in these sacred spaces. The diamond helps the "empire" (now redefined as the global community of ravers and techno lovers) only when it serves to *nourish the calves*—the innocent souls and the devotees.

The **egalitarian prowess** that elevates the diamond to shared public view is Pushan's gift. The security is not to hide the treasure, but to protect it for all. The museum-quality display is not to impress the few, but to inspire the many. The dancehall becomes a sanctuary where the spiritual calves can graze in peace, bathed in the light of the Vajra, free from the wolves of anger and avarice.

From Berghain to Fabric, from Tresor to Output, we envision these spaces transformed: not just dancehalls, but museums where diamonds are both secured and shared. The beats of techno become the rhythm of Pushan's guidance. The communal dance becomes the nourishment of the calves. The Vajra, elevated to public view, feeds the soul of every dancer.
